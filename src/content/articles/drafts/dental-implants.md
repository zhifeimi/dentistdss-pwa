A dental implant is an artificial tooth root, usually made of titanium, that is placed in the jawbone to hold a crown, bridge or denture. Implants are stable, look natural and can last a long time when they are looked after. You need healthy gums and enough bone to support one, and they need the same daily cleaning as natural teeth, because gum disease can still damage the tissue around them.

## How dental implants work

An implant replaces the root of a missing tooth. Once it is placed in the jaw, the bone grows around it and holds it firmly in place. A connector (abutment) is then fitted, and a crown, bridge or denture is attached on top.

Implants can replace:

- **a single tooth**, with one implant and a crown
- **several teeth**, with implants supporting a bridge
- **all the teeth in a jaw**, with implants holding a fixed bridge or a removable denture that clips on

## Implants compared with other options

Missing teeth can also be replaced with a bridge that is attached to the teeth on either side, or with a removable denture, and sometimes leaving the gap is reasonable. Each option suits different situations.

- **Implants** don't rely on the neighbouring teeth, don't move when you eat or speak, and help preserve the jawbone where the tooth was lost. They take longer, involve surgery and usually cost more.
- **Bridges** are fixed in place without surgery, but the teeth on either side are usually prepared to hold them.
- **Dentures** are removable and less invasive, but can move and may need relining or replacing over time.

Your dentist can explain which option fits your mouth, your health and your budget.

## Are you a good candidate?

Implants suit many adults who have lost teeth through decay, gum disease or injury. Your dentist will check:

- **Gum health.** Active gum disease needs to be treated first.
- **Bone.** There needs to be enough bone to hold the implant. If there isn't, a bone graft may be possible.
- **General health.** Conditions that affect healing, such as poorly controlled diabetes, and some medicines need to be considered.
- **Smoking.** Smoking increases the risk of implants failing and of gum problems around them.
- **Age.** Implants are usually placed once the jaw has finished growing.

## What the treatment involves

Treatment usually happens over several months:

1. **Assessment and planning**, with an examination, X-rays or a scan, and a discussion of your options, risks and costs.
2. **Placing the implant**, a minor surgical procedure, usually with local anaesthetic.
3. **Healing**, while the bone grows around the implant. This can take a few months, and you may have a temporary tooth in the meantime.
4. **Fitting the final tooth**, once the implant is secure.

## Risks to discuss

Implant surgery can cause infection, nerve injury, sinus problems or damage to nearby teeth. An implant may fail to join to the bone, and the implant or the teeth attached to it may later need repair or replacement. Ask about your personal risks, the total cost, the treatment timetable and the ongoing maintenance before deciding.

## Looking after your implants

Implants can't decay, but the gum and bone around them can become inflamed and infected, a condition called peri-implantitis. Untreated, it can cause bone loss and the implant can fail. To help your implants last:

- brush twice a day, paying attention to the gumline around the implant
- clean between your teeth and around the implant every day with floss or interdental brushes
- don't smoke
- see your dentist regularly so the implant and gums can be checked and professionally cleaned

## When to see a dentist

Talk to a dentist if you have missing teeth and want to discuss replacing them. If you already have implants, book an appointment if the gum around one bleeds, is swollen or sore, or if the implant or the tooth on it feels loose.
