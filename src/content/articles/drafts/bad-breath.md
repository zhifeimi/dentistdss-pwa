Bad breath, or halitosis, usually starts in the mouth. Bacteria on the teeth, gums and tongue break down food and produce smelly gases. Brushing twice a day, cleaning between your teeth, cleaning your tongue and drinking enough water fix most bad breath. Breath that stays bad despite good care can be a sign of gum disease, decay or a medical problem, so it is worth seeing a dentist.

## What causes bad breath?

Morning breath is normal: saliva flow drops while you sleep, so bacteria multiply. It usually goes once you eat, drink and brush. Bad breath that lasts through the day has other causes:

- **Plaque and food on the teeth and tongue.** Bacteria break down leftover food and release sulphur compounds. The back of the tongue is a common source.
- **Gum disease.** Bacteria in pockets around the teeth produce a strong, lasting odour.
- **Tooth decay and infection**, including food trapped in cavities or around broken fillings.
- **Dry mouth.** Saliva rinses away bacteria and food. Some medicines, breathing through the mouth, not drinking enough water and some medical conditions reduce it.
- **Smoking and vaping**, which dry the mouth, leave their own smell and increase the risk of gum disease.
- **Strong foods and drinks**, such as garlic, onion, coffee and alcohol.
- **Dentures and other appliances** that are not cleaned well.

Less often, bad breath comes from outside the mouth: sinus or throat infections, tonsil stones, reflux, or conditions such as diabetes. Your dentist or doctor can help find the cause.

## How to tell if you have bad breath

It is hard to judge your own breath. You can ask someone you trust, or lick the inside of your wrist, let it dry and smell it. A white or yellow coating on the back of your tongue is another clue.

## How to get rid of bad breath

- **Brush twice a day** for two minutes with fluoride toothpaste.
- **Clean between your teeth every day** with floss or interdental brushes, where food and bacteria build up.
- **Clean your tongue** gently with your toothbrush or a tongue scraper, working from back to front.
- **Drink water through the day** to keep your mouth moist, especially if you take medicines that dry the mouth.
- **Quit smoking.** Your dentist, doctor or Quitline can help.
- **Clean dentures, retainers and mouthguards daily**, and take dentures out at night unless your dentist advises otherwise.
- **Chew sugar-free gum** after meals to boost saliva.
- **Use mouthwash wisely.** It can freshen breath for a while, but it doesn't replace brushing and cleaning between your teeth, and it can hide a problem that needs treatment.

## How a dentist can help

A dentist can check for gum disease, decay, infection and dry mouth, and give your teeth a professional clean to remove plaque and tartar you can't reach at home. If your mouth is healthy and the smell continues, they may suggest seeing your doctor to look for other causes.

## When to see a dentist

Book a check-up if bad breath lasts despite brushing, cleaning between your teeth and cleaning your tongue, or if you also have bleeding or swollen gums, a bad taste, a loose tooth or toothache. See a doctor if bad breath comes with a sore throat, fever, a blocked nose or reflux.
